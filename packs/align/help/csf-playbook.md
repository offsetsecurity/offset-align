# NIST CSF 2.0 playbook

The Cybersecurity Framework is not a checklist and has no certificate. It is a
way to say, in a common language, where you stand, where you intend to be, and
what you are doing about the distance. This is the path, and where each piece
lives here.

Everything below is also in **Get ready**, which tracks how far you have got.

## The shape of it

| Part | What it is | Where |
|---|---|---|
| Core | 6 functions, 22 categories, 106 subcategories (outcomes) | Subcategories |
| Tiers | How deliberately you manage cybersecurity risk, from 1 to 4 | Profile & Tiers |
| Current profile | What you do today, outcome by outcome | Current profile box on each subcategory |
| Target profile | What good enough looks like for you | Target profile box on each subcategory |

The six functions: **GOVERN** (GV), **IDENTIFY** (ID), **PROTECT** (PR),
**DETECT** (DE), **RESPOND** (RS) and **RECOVER** (RC). GOVERN is new in 2.0
and sits over the other five.

## The Tiers

| Tier | Name | In practice |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Partial | Ad hoc, reactive, little awareness of risk |
| 2 | Risk Informed | Management knows the risks, but practice is not organisation-wide |
| 3 | Repeatable | Approved policy, applied consistently, and updated as things change |
| 4 | Adaptive | Improves from every event, and shares what it learns |

Set a current and a target Tier for governance and for risk management
separately.

## Step 1: know what you are protecting

Scope in **Policies**. Your assets in **Assets**, with owners (ID.AM). Who is
accountable for cybersecurity, agreed in writing (GV.RR).

## Step 2: decide how far you want to go

Choose current and target Tiers in **Profile & Tiers**, and note who agreed the
target.

## Step 3: the current profile

For each of the 106 subcategories: a status, an owner, and one sentence on what
really happens today. Mark what does not apply, with a reason.

## Step 4: the target profile

For each one that applies: one sentence on what good enough looks like. Set a
priority where the gap matters.

## Step 5: the risk behind it

**Risks**, scored, owned and decided, and linked to the subcategories that
reduce them. That is what lets you defend the priorities you set in step 4.

## Step 6: prove it, and act on the gap

Evidence for what you say you do, linked and dated. A task for each
high-priority gap, with an owner and a date. The **Gap report** is your action
plan, and the **Executive summary** is what you show the board.

## What people ask for, and where it lives

| Asked for | Where |
|---|---|
| Your scope and what you depend on | Policies, Assets |
| Who is accountable | Subcategory owners, GV.RR |
| Your current and target Tiers | Profile & Tiers |
| Your current and target profiles | Subcategories, and the Gap report |
| Your risk register and how you decide | Risks, and the risk register report |
| Proof behind a claim | Evidence, and the evidence register report |
| What you are doing about the gaps | Tasks, and the Readiness plan report |
| How you handled incidents | Incidents, Findings |
| Board-level picture | Executive summary report |
